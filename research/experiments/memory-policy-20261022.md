# Sequential memory organization and allocation study

1. Compact motion state, retaining immutable route data in canonical buffers. Charge extraction, common radix/context indexes, motion, junctions and synchronization. Gate: exact state/summary equivalence and >=5% aggregate replayed-step improvement, repeatable across three process batches; verify all nine frozen 600-second trajectories before freezing a new baseline.
2. Persistent compact storage / slot mapping against that frozen baseline, including initialization, refresh and migration costs. Distinguish storage retained across evolving steps from a supplied immutable snapshot.
3. Selective additional local lookup structures and shared-memory staging, measured independently against the same baseline. Keep mandatory correctness indexes/protections active. Measure construction and update costs and unused capacity. Require exact physical results.
4. Fixed additional-memory budgets: compare uniform, vehicle-count, cheap observed-state and prior-predicted savings-per-byte selections. Fit using distributed/concentrated demand only; reserve bursty demand and a fixed subset of regions for testing. Include selection and maintenance costs. Report a measured-cost oracle candidate separately; do not call a post-hoc rule a prior. Any universally losing candidate remains a negative result, not a deployed optimization.

Frozen input: Berkeley block-imbalance milestone, 2,048/8,192/16,384 requested trips, four matched windows per pattern. Three process repeats, three warmups, ten randomized timed repeats. No hardware-counter claims without actual profiles. Additional memory, population, output checks and build/math settings remain explicit. Final tables distinguish full trajectory validation, immutable snapshots and derived optimistic bounds. Earlier milestones remain untouched.
